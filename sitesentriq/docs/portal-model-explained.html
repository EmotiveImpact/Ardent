Who sees what? The platform, the operator, and their clients

A plain-English + visual answer to: what is a “client”, who logs into the portal, where the security gap is, and why we’re binding each portal login to one client.

1 Three layers: the platform, your customer, and their customers

The word “client” is overloaded, so here is exactly who is who in Haumea.

So “client” = which one?

A client is the customer of your customer — i.e. the customer of the platform’s customer. Haumea’s paying customer is the FM operator (the organisation). That operator does facilities work for several clients (Acme, Beta, Gamma). Those clients are not Haumea’s customers — they’re the operator’s.

2 Who logs in, and what they’re meant to see

Login type (role)Who they areShould see
staff
org_admin, scheduler, finance, engineer…		 Employees of the FM operator (Apex) Everything in their organisation — all clients, all properties, all jobs. This is correct.
client_admin A person at one of the operator’s clients (e.g. the facilities manager at Acme Retail) Only their own client’s properties, compliance, visits, quotes & invoices — via the /portal Client Hub.
tenant_user A single-site contact (e.g. the manager of one Acme shop) Only their one property — via /site. (This one is already correctly locked down.)

3 The security gap we found

Today, a client_admin is scoped only to the organisation — not to their client. So Acme’s manager can see Beta’s and Gamma’s data too.

allowed / correct leaked / should be blocked

Why: the database rule (RLS) on quotes/invoices/properties says only organisation_id = my org. Jane’s org is Apex (the operator), the same org Beta & Gamma sit under — so the rule lets her through. There is no “…and only my client” part, because a portal user isn’t tied to a client at all yet.

4 The fix — bind each portal login to one client (Model 1)

We add one column — profiles.client_id — so each client_admin points at exactly one client. Then the rule becomes “my org AND row.client_id = my client”.

Staff logins are unaffected — the “only my client” part only applies to client_admin. Everyone else keeps the full org view.

Why Model 1 (one client per user) and not Model 2?

Model 2 (one login can manage several clients) only earns its extra complexity — a join table + a client-switcher in the UI + more intricate rules — if you genuinely have managing agents: one person who administers Acme and Beta and Gamma. For a standard portal where each customer org has its own login, Model 1 is simpler, safer, and you can upgrade to Model 2 later without redoing the work. Unless you know you have multi-client agents, Model 1 is the right call.

5 What “backfill” means (and why I asked)

When we turn on the rule “you only see your client’s data”, every existing client_admin login needs to already know which client it belongs to (its client_id). If a login’s client_id is blank when the rule goes live, the rule blocks everything for them — they’d be locked out of their own data.

“Backfill” = filling in that client_id for the logins that already exist in the live database, before the rule switches on. Three situations:

  • No client_admins live yet — the Client Hub isn’t in real use with customer logins, so there’s nothing to fill in. We ship the column + rule and it’s enforced from the first portal user you ever create. (For an MVP this is usually the case.)
  • You provide the mapping — there ARE real portal logins; you tell me “jane@acme.com → Acme, sam@beta.com → Beta” and I bake that into the change so nobody loses access.
  • Infer by email domain — I best-effort match each login to a client by email domain, for you to check before it runs.

That’s the whole question: “for the portal logins that already exist, who belongs to whom?” If none exist yet, the answer is simply “none — ship it.”

6 Billing — your question about charging per client

Two different money flows, don’t mix them up:

Money flowWho pays whomWhere in Haumea
Operator → their clients Apex invoices Acme/Beta/Gamma for the facilities work it does (call-outs, PPM, repairs). The quotes & invoices features. Yes — the operator invoices their clients, exactly as you guessed.
You → the operator Apex pays Haumea a subscription to use the platform. The Stripe integration (stripe-webhook, subscriptions) — billed per organisation, i.e. one subscription per FM operator.

“Do we charge per client they add?”

That’s a pricing-model decision you own — not something the code forces. Today the subscription is per-operator (per organisation), not per client. Adding a client doesn’t change what they pay Haumea. If you want per-client (or per-property, or per-seat) pricing, that’s a deliberate product change we’d build on top — it isn’t wired in now, and the RLS fix above doesn’t depend on it either way. Worth deciding separately from the security fix.
