# Discovery Interview Guide — UK FM Operators

A 30-minute structured conversation designed to tell us, in one call, whether SiteSentriq solves a real problem worth paying for.

**Critical rule: no demo in call one.** A demo derails discovery. The prospect starts evaluating the product instead of telling you about their world. Discovery first; demo on a separate call.

---

## Before the call

### Send 1 hour before
A calendar invite with:
- A single Google Meet / Zoom link
- A one-line agenda: *"30 minutes to understand how your team handles compliance and PPM today — no pitch, no demo, just questions about your world."*
- Your real name + phone number for fallback

### Have open in tabs
- A blank Google Doc / Apple Notes — for verbatim notes
- This guide
- Their company's website + LinkedIn page (referenced if needed, not pitched at)

### What you're optimising for
- **Verbatim quotes** about pain. The phrases they use are the phrases you'll put on the website.
- **A clear yes/no on whether continuous-compliance-score resonates.** If it does, you have product-market fit signal. If it doesn't on 5/5 calls, you've built the wrong thing.
- **A real next step.** "Would you like to see it?" → yes = call two booked. No = warm "thank you, we may circle back". Either is fine.

### What you're NOT trying to do
- Pitch.
- Sell.
- Defend the product.
- Demonstrate features.
- Quote pricing.

If they ask "so what does it do?" mid-discovery, the right answer is *"I'd love to show you on a second call once I understand your world better — for now, can I ask…"* and you return to the questions.

---

## The 7 questions

Ask them in order. Each one has follow-ups depending on what they say. Don't read them verbatim — let it flow like a conversation. But cover all seven.

---

### Q1 — Current state of compliance evidence

> *"To kick off — can you walk me through how your team handles compliance evidence today? When an auditor, insurer, or a big client asks for proof that statutory work is up to date, what's the actual flow from request to evidence-in-hand?"*

**Why we're asking.** This surfaces their real workflow and what we'd be replacing. The detail of the answer also tells you how seriously they take compliance vs treating it as an afterthought.

**Listen for (good signal):**
- "I have to chase the engineer for the cert"
- "It lives in a shared drive somewhere"
- "It takes me 2-3 days when an audit comes in"
- "We've got [Joblogic / Trackplan / Pirana / Maintenance Connection] but the reporting is rubbish"
- "I cobble it together from spreadsheets and emails"

**Listen for (bad signal):**
- "It's fine, we have a system that handles it"
- *(speed: instant, confident, dismissive)*

**Follow-ups if they're vague:**
- "When was the last time someone actually asked you for compliance evidence — who was it and what happened?"
- "If your top client emailed you right now asking for proof of fire alarm servicing across all sites in the last 12 months, what would you do in the next hour?"

---

### Q2 — The moment of pain

> *"What's the worst compliance-related moment you've had in the past 12 months? An audit that went badly, a near-miss, a lost contract, a regulator letter, a customer escalation?"*

**Why we're asking.** Pain frequency × magnitude → willingness to pay. People don't pay to prevent abstract risk; they pay to prevent the specific thing that happened to them last year.

**Listen for (good signal):**
- A specific incident with a date and a name
- A specific financial number ("we lost the £X contract because…")
- A regulator or insurance-driven event ("HSE got involved", "our broker raised the premium 30%")
- Visible discomfort recalling it — that's the buying nerve

**Listen for (bad signal):**
- "Touch wood, we've never really had an issue"
- Vague generalities — "compliance is always a bit stressful"

**Follow-up:**
- "How often does something like that happen — once a year, twice, more?"
- "What did it actually cost you, if you had to put a number on it?"

---

### Q3 — The continuous score idea (this is the differentiator test)

> *"Here's a what-if — and tell me honestly, useful or 'we already have that' or 'why would I care'. What if you had a single number, 0-100, that scored your compliance health across every site and every client, updated every night automatically — and a public URL you could send to insurers or top clients so they could verify the score themselves without logging into anything. Does that land for you?"*

**Why we're asking.** This is the direct test of the Continuous Compliance Score + public Trust URL — the actual differentiator. Their reaction here is the single most important data point of the entire call.

**Listen for (good signal — they're a buyer):**
- "Wait, you can do that?"
- "Show me what that looks like" *(invitation to call two)*
- Specific use case unprompted — "I'd send that to [insurer name]"
- They ask how it's calculated
- They ask who else has it / what competitors offer this

**Listen for (qualified signal):**
- "Interesting" *(noncommittal — probe further)*
- "We'd need to verify the calculation methodology"
- "Our insurer doesn't ask for that"

**Listen for (bad signal — wrong differentiator):**
- A shrug
- "We just need the certificates, not a score"
- "Our compliance is already audited annually, I don't need a daily number"

**Follow-up:**
- *If positive:* "If you had that today, who's the first person you'd share the URL with?"
- *If lukewarm:* "What WOULD make this useful — what would the number have to do for you to use it?"
- *If negative:* "What would be useful instead? Forget what I just said — what would actually move the needle for your compliance workflow?"

---

### Q4 — Buyer and budget

> *"Practical question — if you decided to buy something like this, who'd actually sign the cheque? You, your boss, ops director, the owner? And what's the budget bracket you'd be working with — under £500 a month, £500-2k, £2k-5k, more than that?"*

**Why we're asking.** Identifies the decision-maker (a single named person = short sales cycle; "procurement" = long) and gives you a price ceiling to design around. Most operators will engage honestly on this — they'd rather tell you upfront than waste their time.

**Listen for (good signal):**
- A single name or role
- A specific bracket they engage with
- "Yeah we already spend about £X a month on [current system]" — that's your benchmark

**Listen for (red flag):**
- "Procurement gets involved" *(6-month sales cycle minimum — qualify ruthlessly)*
- "We don't have a budget for software" *(may be true; may be code for "not interested" — probe)*
- A bracket dramatically lower than your target pricing

**Follow-up:**
- "What are you currently paying for [whatever they named as current system]?"
- "What would have to be true for the budget bracket to go up?"

---

### Q5 — Current system and switching cost

> *"What are you using today? And — honestly — what would have to be true for you to switch? Or what would block the switch entirely?"*

**Why we're asking.** Identifies your competitor for this specific deal, and surfaces the dealbreakers. Many operators are 6-18 months into a 3-year contract with [Joblogic / Trackplan / etc] and the switching cost is dominant.

**Listen for (good signal):**
- Contract ending soon
- Specific frustrations with current vendor ("their mobile app is terrible")
- "We've been meaning to look at alternatives"
- "Honestly we're winging it with spreadsheets" *(highest-conversion segment)*

**Listen for (bad signal — disqualify gracefully):**
- "We just signed a 3-year deal with [X] six months ago"
- "Our parent company mandates [enterprise CMMS]"
- "Migration would be impossible — we have 5 years of data in [X]"

**Follow-up:**
- "If you did switch, what's the migration headache you'd worry about most?"
- "What does your current vendor do that you'd hate to lose?"

---

### Q6 — The "no"

> *"Here's a useful one. If you finished this call and decided you were NOT going to take this further — walk me through what the reason would be. What are you sceptical about?"*

**Why we're asking.** Pre-empts objections, surfaces the real blockers they'd never tell you unprompted. Phrased as "your reason for no" it bypasses politeness — they'll tell you the truth.

**Listen for (good signal — addressable objections):**
- Specific feature gap ("you don't seem to do gas certificates")
- Specific concern ("our engineers won't use a new mobile app, last one bombed")
- Specific evidence need ("I'd need to see it work with our actual data")

**Listen for (bad signal — unaddressable):**
- "Just doesn't feel right"
- "We're too busy right now to change anything"
- "I'd need approval from people who aren't on this call"

**Follow-up:**
- *If they raised a feature gap:* "If we did do X, would that change your answer?"
- *If they raised concern:* "What would you need to see to feel comfortable about that?"

---

### Q7 — The bridge to call two

> *"Last one. You've been generous with your time — based on what we've talked about, would you be open to a 20-minute follow-up where I walk you through what we built and you can poke at the live trust URL for your shape of operation? No commitment. If it lands, great; if not, you've helped us understand the market."*

**Why we're asking.** Tests buying intent. Their answer is the call's verdict.

**Listen for:**
- *"Yes, send me a slot for next week"* — warm. Book it inside this call. Don't say "I'll send something over" — get the calendar invite out before you hang up.
- *"Maybe — send me some info first"* — qualified. Send the `narrative/one-page-narrative.md` PDF + the public Trust URL link as a sample. Follow up in 5 days per template.
- *"No, thanks — not for us right now"* — cold. Thank them warmly, ask if you can circle back in 6 months. They are a data point, not a failure.

**End the call:**
- *"Genuinely, thank you. Two final things — first, what would be the worst possible compliance system feature, the one that would make you hate using software? Helps us know what to avoid. Second, anyone else in the UK FM space you'd point me at, even if it's a competitor — I'm trying to understand the market, not just sell something."*

The second question is gold. Operators talk to each other. One referral is worth 20 cold outreaches.

## After the call

### Within 1 hour
- Send a thank-you email (3 lines max). Include the public Trust URL link as something to play with.
- Copy your raw verbatim notes into `docs/sales/findings/round-1-call-N-{org-name-anonymised}.md`.

### Within 24 hours
- Score the call on the rubric below.
- If positive, book the demo. If lukewarm, send the one-pager and queue follow-up.

### The rubric (be honest)

For each call, score:

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real pain identified | None | Generic | A bit | Clear | Visceral |
| Score idea resonated | Shrug | Polite | Curious | Engaged | "Wait, you can do that?" |
| Buyer signal | None | "Maybe one day" | Vague interest | Specific next step | "What's the URL, what's the price" |
| Budget realistic | <£100/mo | <£300/mo | £300-1k | £1-3k | £3k+ |
| Switching cost | Locked in | High | Medium | Low | Spreadsheets |

Calls scoring **20+ across all five rows** are your first-customer candidates. Calls scoring **<12** are noise — don't over-index on them.

---

## After 5 calls

When you have 5 completed discovery calls:

1. Re-read every verbatim note set.
2. Look for **3+ operators using the same phrase** about a pain point — that's your messaging.
3. Look for **3+ operators dismissing the same feature** — that's a feature to consider cutting.
4. Look for **the question 3+ operators asked unprompted** — that's the FAQ on your website.
5. Write `docs/sales/findings/round-1-summary.md` with the patterns.
6. Decide: do round two with the same materials, or rewrite the one-pager/demo using the new language and re-do outreach?
