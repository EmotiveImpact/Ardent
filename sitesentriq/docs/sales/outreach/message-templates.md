# Cold Outreach Message Templates — UK FM

Five templates, in the order you'll typically use them. Each is calibrated for UK FM operators, not US SaaS prospects. UK operators respond to specificity, warmth, and brevity — not to "10x" language or pushy CTAs.

---

## Template 1 — Cold LinkedIn DM (~140 words)

The first touch. Goal: get one reply, any reply. Not to book a call yet.

```
Hi [first name] —

Augustus here, founder at SiteSentriq. We've built a UK FM platform with one thing nobody else has — a continuous compliance score that updates nightly and a public "trust URL" you can share with insurers, top clients, or in tender responses to prove compliance live.

I'm doing customer-discovery calls with 20 UK FM operators this month — your name came up [via IWFM / through a search for compliance-focused operators in your region / via FMJ's recent profile of you / honestly: cold off LinkedIn].

Not pitching. Trying to understand how operators like [Their Company] handle compliance evidence today, and whether what we've built solves a real problem or solves the wrong problem. 30-minute call, no demo.

If that sounds useful — or if you'd point me at someone better placed — I'd be grateful.

Augustus
```

**Personalisation slots to fill before sending:**
- `[first name]` — never "Hi there"
- `[via IWFM / FMJ / ...]` — pick one sourcing reason; honesty about cold off LinkedIn beats fake warmth
- `[Their Company]` — full company name

**What works in this template:**
- Names them, the platform, what's different — in the first sentence
- Honest about it being customer discovery, not a sales pitch
- "Or point me at someone better" gives them an easy out that costs them nothing
- 140 words. Anything longer gets skimmed and binned.

**What to NEVER do in template 1:**
- Pitch features
- Quote pricing
- Send a calendar link in DM 1 (creates pressure; they need to choose to engage first)
- Use the words "synergy", "leverage", "transform", "revolutionise" — all immediate bin
- Use emojis in a UK B2B FM context — different culture from US SaaS

## Template 2 — Cold Email (~200 words)

Used when LinkedIn DM isn't an option (no profile, no first-degree path) or as a follow-up to a LinkedIn DM that got read but not replied.

Subject lines (rotate, A/B test):
- `Quick question about how [Their Company] handles compliance evidence`
- `Customer-discovery call — UK FM operator perspective?`
- `Trust URLs for FM operators — would value 30 min of your time`

```
Hi [first name],

Augustus Edem here — I founded SiteSentriq, a UK FM platform that puts a continuous compliance score and a public verification URL at the core, rather than per-asset traffic-light reports.

Most UK FM operators I've researched are juggling 8-12 statutory compliance regimes across multiple clients, where the work isn't the hard part — proving the work to auditors, insurers, and top clients is. Everyone has Gas Safe certificates somewhere. Almost nobody can produce a live org-wide compliance score for an insurance broker on demand.

We've built that. EU-West-2 hosted, ICO-aligned, no per-asset busywork. Differentiated from Joblogic/Trackplan/Pirana on continuous monitoring rather than snapshot reporting.

I'm doing 20 customer-discovery calls this month with UK FM operators in the 5-50 engineer range. The goal of the call is to understand your world, not to pitch. 30 minutes, your questions as much as mine. If it lands, we book a demo separately. If it doesn't, you've given me data and I leave you alone.

Would the week of [date] work for a 30-min slot? I can fit around your day.

Either way, here's a live example of the trust URL I mentioned: [demo trust URL]. Refresh it; it really is live.

Augustus
[phone] · [email]
```

**Personalisation slots:**
- `[Their Company]` if you can reference something specific from their site / case study
- `[date]` — pick a week 5-10 working days out, not "tomorrow"

**Why this works:**
- Earns the right to ask by demonstrating market understanding first
- Names competitors directly — UK operators respect knowing the landscape
- The live trust URL embedded is the "show, don't tell" wedge. They'll click it.
- Hard offer of a specific week beats "let me know when works"

## Template 3 — Warm Intro Request (asking a mutual contact)

When you've identified a target but a mutual LinkedIn connection knows them. 10x conversion rate vs cold.

```
Hi [mutual contact's first name],

Hope you're well. Quick favour to ask.

I noticed you're connected to [target's first name + last name] at [target's company]. I'm building SiteSentriq — a UK FM platform with a continuous compliance score and a public trust URL — and I'm doing 20 customer-discovery calls with UK FM operators this month to make sure we've built what the market actually wants.

[Target's company] looks like a strong fit for the discovery list. Would you be open to forwarding a short intro to [target's first name]? I've drafted it below for ease — feel free to edit or rewrite entirely:

---

[Target first name] — meet Augustus Edem, founder of SiteSentriq. He's building a UK FM platform that puts continuous compliance scoring and public verification URLs at the core — designed for operators like [target's company] who are tired of cobbling compliance evidence together for tenders and insurers. He's doing customer-discovery calls (not pitching) and I thought you two should talk. I'll let you take it from there.

---

If you'd rather not — totally fine, no pressure at all. If you would, I really appreciate it.

Augustus
```

**When NOT to use this:**
- If the mutual is a weak connection or you haven't talked in years (creates awkwardness for them)
- If you've already DM'd the target cold (don't send intro AND cold; pick one)
- More than once per mutual per quarter — don't burn the connection

## Template 4 — Follow-up #1 (5 working days after no reply)

If they read your DM/email and didn't reply (LinkedIn shows read receipts; email needs a tracker like Mailtrack), wait a full week then try once more.

```
Hi [first name],

Following up gently on my note last week about the customer-discovery call. Totally understand if the timing's wrong — wanted to make sure my last message didn't get buried, which I know happens.

One specific thing that might help: here's a 90-second video walking through the trust URL I mentioned: [Loom link]. Even if a call isn't right for you, you'll see what's different in the time it takes to make a coffee.

If the answer's a clear no, just reply "no thanks" and I won't message again. If it's a "not now", let me know when's better and I'll circle back then.

Augustus
```

**Why this works:**
- "Not now" + offer of a specific later date converts ~30% of non-responders to a future yes
- The "reply 'no thanks' and I won't message again" is the ethical opt-out — UK operators respect this enormously vs. relentless follow-up
- The 90-second Loom is the show-don't-tell upgrade if they preferred not to commit to a call

---

## Template 5 — Follow-up #2 (12 working days after #1, soft close)

The last touch. If they didn't reply to #1, send this then move on.

```
Hi [first name],

Last note from me — promise. If now's not the right time, no offence taken.

If anything changes in the next 6 months and you want to see what continuous compliance scoring could look like for [Their Company], my email and number are below. The trust URL stays live regardless: [demo trust URL].

All the best with everything at [Their Company],

Augustus
[phone] · [email]
```

**The "last note from me — promise" line is critical.** It signals that you're respectful of their time and won't keep pestering. Counterintuitively, this often gets a reply when the previous two didn't, because it removes the pressure they were avoiding.

After this template: move on. Don't message again. Add them to "circle back in 6 months" list in your spreadsheet.

## The do / don't list

### DO
- ✅ Personalise the first sentence (name + company + one specific thing about them)
- ✅ Be honest about cold outreach when you have no warm angle
- ✅ Lead with what's different about the product (compliance score + trust URL), not feature lists
- ✅ Use British English spelling (compliance not compliance, organisation not organization)
- ✅ Give them an easy "no" — UK operators respect being given an out
- ✅ Keep all messages under 200 words
- ✅ Send messages between 8:30am and 10am UK time on Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday for highest reply rates
- ✅ Reply to any response within 2 hours during work hours

### DO NOT
- ❌ Use US SaaS language ("10x", "transform", "leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem", "growth hacking")
- ❌ Send a Calendly link in message 1 (it screams "you're prospect #47 in my CRM")
- ❌ Use emojis in B2B FM cold outreach (different culture from US SaaS)
- ❌ Pitch features in message 1 — discovery only
- ❌ Quote pricing in messages — that's call-two territory
- ❌ Send more than 3 messages to the same person across all templates
- ❌ Reply with hurt feelings or sarcasm to a "no" — burn no bridges, ever
- ❌ Send on Friday afternoons, weekends, or UK bank holidays
- ❌ Send the same template to multiple people at the same company in the same week (very visible if they compare notes)

## Realistic response rates

For round one, expect:

| Activity | Volume | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Cold messages sent | 20 | — |
| Any reply | 4-8 | 20-40% (good for cold, plausible if templates are personalised) |
| Discovery calls booked | 2-4 | 10-20% of total sent |
| Discovery calls completed | 2-4 | ~100% of booked |
| Demos done | 1-3 | 50-75% of discovery |
| Paying customers | 0-2 | 0-10% of total sent |

If response rate is **under 10%** of total sent: change the message angle. Read it again. The first sentence isn't doing its job, or you're targeting the wrong segment.

If response rate is **above 30%** of total sent: you've nailed the angle. Scale to 50 in round two.

## Reply triage — what to do with each kind of response

| Reply type | What to do |
|---|---|
| *"Sure, send a Calendly link"* | Send link immediately. Confirm slot within 24h. Save calendar invite to your calendar with full prep notes attached. |
| *"What does it do specifically?"* | Send the `narrative/one-page-narrative.md` as a PDF. Repeat the call ask at the end. Don't try to qualify them in writing. |
| *"How much does it cost?"* | Reply: *"Depends on your operation — full pricing on the call. Round figures are £499/mo base for up to 10 sites scaling up from there, but I'd rather understand your shape first so I can give you a real number not a brochure number."* |
| *"We're happy with our current system."* | Reply: *"Totally understood. Two-line question if you don't mind — what's the one thing you wish [current system] did that it doesn't? Asking for product reasons, no follow-up unless you want one."* (Sometimes converts to a discovery call. Sometimes gives you free competitive intel either way.) |
| *"Not interested, thanks."* | Reply once: *"Appreciate the honest answer. If anything changes, I'm around. All the best with everything at [their company]."* Then never message again. |
| Silence after follow-up #2 | Move on. Add to 6-month circle-back list. |
| *"Tell me more"* (vague) | Send one-pager + offer specific call slot. Don't ramble in writing. |
| *"Send a demo video"* | Send 90-second Loom of the trust URL. Repeat the call ask. Don't ship a 30-min recorded demo — they won't watch it and you've spent the bullet. |
| Critical / cynical reply | Reply warmly and substantively to the criticism. Cynics who get a non-defensive response convert at unusually high rates. Don't argue back. |

## Sending cadence for round one (3-week schedule)

| Week | Day | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon | Build 20-target list (see `target-finding-playbook.md`) |
| 1 | Tue | Draft personalised opening line for each of 20 |
| 1 | Wed-Thu | Send 10 LinkedIn DMs + 10 cold emails (split test) |
| 1 | Fri | Reply to any responses received |
| 2 | Mon | Send follow-up #1 to non-responders from week 1 |
| 2 | Tue-Thu | Run discovery calls as they get booked |
| 3 | Mon | Send follow-up #2 to remaining non-responders |
| 3 | Tue-Fri | Finish discovery calls + run any demos booked |
| 3 | Fri | Synthesise findings into `docs/sales/findings/round-1-summary.md` |

Round one ends end of week 3. Pause. Read findings. Decide round two strategy.
