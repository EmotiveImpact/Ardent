# Target-Finding Playbook — 20 UK FM Operators

How to build a list of 20 qualified UK facilities-management operators worth cold-outreaching for round one of customer discovery.

**Time estimate:** 2-3 hours.
**Output:** a spreadsheet (CSV / Google Sheets / Notion table) with 20 rows, each containing the columns at the bottom of this document.

---

## The ideal customer profile

Before searching, anchor on what "good" looks like. Round-one targets should match most of:

- **Size:** 5–50 engineers; 20–500 sites under management; £1M–£20M revenue
- **Geography:** UK or Ireland (data residency matters; ICO-aligned)
- **Work mix:** mix of planned PPM and reactive callouts; statutory compliance pressure (gas, electrical, fire, water, lifts)
- **Client mix:** at least some regulated or insurance-sensitive clients (NHS, councils, housing associations, retail chains, regulated landlords, schools, care homes)
- **Software situation:** either (a) using a legacy CMMS they complain about, or (b) cobbling things together with spreadsheets/emails
- **Decision-maker accessible:** ideally a founder, MD, or ops director you can reach directly on LinkedIn — not a 500-person company with a procurement function

**Avoid for round one:**
- In-house single-site facility managers (different buying motion)
- Enterprise mega-contractors using Maximo/Planon (long sales cycle, wrong segment)
- Pure-reactive callout-only shops with no PPM book (the compliance story doesn't land)
- Companies <5 engineers (too small to need the platform; they'll cope with spreadsheets)

## The 8 sourcing channels (in priority order)

### 1. IWFM Member Directory — highest signal

The Institute of Workplace and Facilities Management is the UK's primary FM professional body. Members are operators, not enterprise giants.

- **URL:** https://www.iwfm.org.uk
- **What to look for:** Corporate Members section, filter by company type "FM Service Provider" or "Managing Agent"
- **What to capture per row:** Company name, registered office, headline service description
- **Pro-tip:** Cross-reference each company against LinkedIn to find the MD or Operations Director by name

### 2. BESA Member List

Building Engineering Services Association — covers HVAC, plumbing, building services. Many M&E-leaning FM operators are members.

- **URL:** https://www.thebesa.com
- **Look for:** Companies tagged "Service & Maintenance" with engineer counts ≤ 50

### 3. LinkedIn Sales Navigator — most reliable scale

If you have a Sales Navigator subscription (worth the £79/mo for one month of round-one work):

- **Search 1:** Title: "Managing Director" OR "Operations Director" OR "Founder". Industry: "Facilities Services". Geography: United Kingdom. Company size: 11-200. Years in role: 2+.
- **Search 2:** Title: "Compliance Manager" OR "Quality Manager". Industry: "Facilities Services". Geography: UK. Company size: 11-200.
- **Search 3:** Title: "Field Service Manager" OR "Service Manager". Industry: "Facilities Services". UK. 11-200.

Save the top 30-50 results. Filter manually for ICP fit. Aim for 20 high-quality matches.

If no Sales Navigator: do the same searches on regular LinkedIn — less filterable but free.

### 4. Companies House

Free, comprehensive, UK-only. The "advanced search" lets you filter by SIC code.

- **URL:** https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk
- **SIC codes that match FM operators:**
  - 81100 — Combined facilities support activities
  - 81210 — General cleaning of buildings
  - 81229 — Other building and industrial cleaning activities
  - 80100 — Private security activities
  - 33140 — Repair of electrical equipment
  - 43220 — Plumbing, heat and air-conditioning installation
- **Filter to:** Active companies, registered 5-30 years ago (avoid brand-new and ancient)
- **Cross-reference:** Each company on LinkedIn to find people + verify operator vs single-trade

### 5. Trade Publications — for warm leads

Three UK FM publications. Their advertiser/sponsor lists are pre-filtered for marketing-aware operators (i.e. they value their brand and would care about the trust URL story).

- **FMJ** (Facilities Management Journal) — https://www.fmj.co.uk — Look at recent issue advertisers and case-study subjects
- **PFM** (Premises & Facilities Management) — https://www.pfmonthemove.com
- **Facilitate** (IWFM's magazine) — https://www.facilitatemagazine.com — Case-study subjects are gold; they're operators happy to be quoted, so they'll respond to outreach

Read 3-4 recent issues, capture every operator mentioned in a case study or article.

### 6. Trade-Body Certified Contractor Directories

Each compliance regime has a register. Companies on those registers are by definition compliance-aware — exactly your buyer.

- **Gas Safe Register:** https://www.gassaferegister.co.uk — search by trade and area
- **NICEIC Roll:** https://www.niceic.com — registered electrical contractors
- **Legionella Control Association:** https://www.legionellacontrol.org.uk/membership
- **F-Gas registered companies:** https://www.refcom.org.uk

Look for companies on multiple registers — those are full-service FM operators, not single-trade.

### 7. Regional FM Trade Groups

The big national bodies have regional sub-groups, often more accessible than national events.

- **RICS Facilities Management:** https://www.rics.org — regional events list
- **BIFM/IWFM Regional Groups:** Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow, Bristol regional pages
- **Asset Skills:** the FM sector skills body, has a contractor list

Attendees of recent regional events = current operators with budget and bandwidth for new things.

### 8. Conference Attendee Lists

- **Workplace Trends:** https://workplacetrends.co — major UK FM/workplace conference
- **Facilities Show (part of UK Construction Week):** annual, large attendance
- **The Facilities Event:** smaller, more operator-focused
- **CIBSE Build2Perform:** building services and FM crossover

Most conferences publish attendee/sponsor lists. Sometimes you can ask the conference organiser for a partner-rate attendee list — depends on the event.

## How to qualify a row before adding it to the list

For each potential target, spend 5-10 minutes confirming:

| Check | Where | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Right size | LinkedIn company page | 11-200 employees |
| Active in FM | LinkedIn About + website | Multiple service lines, repeat client testimonials, recent case studies |
| Real operations | Companies House filings | Active accounts, growing revenue, not insolvent |
| Compliance pressure | Service list, accreditations | Holds at least 2 compliance accreditations (Gas Safe, NICEIC, Legionella, etc.) |
| Accessible decision-maker | LinkedIn | MD/Founder/Ops Director with a real profile (photo, recent activity, 100+ connections) |
| Not a competitor's customer | Recent LinkedIn posts | Don't see them publicly raving about Joblogic/Trackplan |

Skip the row if it fails ≥2 checks. Quality > quantity for round one.

## The 20-target target

Build the list to exactly 20 rows for round one. Not 10 (too small a sample), not 50 (you won't follow up properly).

After round one:
- If conversion is 1+ paying customer: keep the list, add 20 more for round two.
- If conversion is 0 customers but 5+ discovery calls done: re-target the next 20 with what you learned from round one (different ICP, different segment, different size).
- If conversion is 0 calls done: don't build round-two list yet. The blocker is outreach effectiveness, not target quality — re-read `message-templates.md` and tighten the angle.

---

## A note on referrals

End every discovery call with the question from `discovery/interview-guide.md`:

> *"Anyone else in UK FM you'd point me at — even competitors?"*

One referral converts ~10x better than one cold message. If 2/5 round-one calls give you a referral, your round-two list is half-built by warm intros — much easier work.

## Time-budget for round one

| Activity | Time |
|---|---|
| Sourcing + qualifying the 20-target list | 2-3 hours |
| Drafting personalised outreach for each (using templates as base) | 2 hours |
| Sending all 20 messages | 1 hour |
| Following up on replies + booking calls | 3-4 hours over 2 weeks |
| Conducting 4-6 discovery calls @ 30 min each + 60 min note-taking | 6-8 hours |
| 2-3 demo calls @ 20 min each + 40 min follow-up | 3-4 hours |
| Synthesising findings | 2 hours |

**Total: ~20-25 hours over 3 weeks.** That's the realistic shape of one customer-development round. Don't compress it; the calendar-thinking time between calls is part of the work.
