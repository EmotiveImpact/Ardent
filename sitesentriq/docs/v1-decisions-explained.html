Two decisions, explained

Neither is broken or blocking — both are small choices about how you want things to behave. Here's exactly what you're choosing between, in plain English.

A When does your customer get their certificate email?

When an engineer finishes a compliance job, the app can automatically email the customer their certificate. The only question is: which moment triggers that email?

Your app has two "the job is finished" statuses:

StatusWhat it means
PerformedThe engineer physically did the work on site.
CompletedThe job is fully signed off.

Right now, the certificate email only fires on "Completed." Marking a job "Performed" sends the customer nothing.

The restaurant analogy.
🍳 "Performed"
The chef has finished cooking the meal.
✅ "Completed"
The manager checked the plate before it leaves the kitchen.
Your choice is simply: do you want a manager-check step before the customer gets their certificate, or not?

Option 1 — Keep it as-is  Recommended

The engineer marks "Performed" in the field. Someone in your office reviews the cert & photos, then marks "Completed" — and that emails the certificate to the customer. There's a human check before a legal document goes out.

Option 2 — Let "Performed" also send

The moment the engineer marks the job done on site, the customer instantly gets their certificate. Faster and fewer steps — but no review step in between.

The real-world trade-off. An engineer finishes a fire-alarm test but forgets to upload the photos and marks it done.
  • With Option 1, your office catches it before the customer ever sees an incomplete certificate.
  • With Option 2, the customer has already received a half-finished compliance document.
Recommendation. Keep Option 1. A compliance certificate is a legal record — most FM businesses want that office check. Only switch to Option 2 if your engineers are the final word with no back-office review. It's a tiny change either way, whenever you decide.

B How automatic should multi-property invoicing be?

Your customer wants one client (e.g. "Acme Retail") with many properties (their 5 shops) billed together on one invoice, while each property keeps its own number (P-101, P-102…).

The key thing: you can already do this today. An invoice is attached to a client, and you can add as many line items as you like — so you can bill all 5 shops on one invoice right now. The catch is you type those lines in by hand.

What doesn't exist yet is a one-click generator that gathers every finished, not-yet-billed job across all of Acme's shops and builds the invoice for you — each line tagged with its P- number.

Option 1 — Leave it manual

Already works. You hand-type the lines. Zero new work. Fine if you only bill multiple sites occasionally.

Option 2 — Build the full auto-generator  ~1–2 days

A "Generate consolidated invoice" button on each client that gathers all their completed, un-billed jobs across every property and builds the line items automatically. This is the literal thing your customer described.

Option 3 — A lighter middle ground

A button on the invoice form that lets you pull in a client's finished jobs as pre-filled lines (no typing), but stops short of the full automatic gather. Roughly half the work of Option 2, covers most of the benefit.

The real-world trade-off — it comes down to volume.
  • Billing dozens of sites every month? Hand-typing is exactly the pain your customer flagged → Option 2 pays for itself fast.
  • Billing multiple sites only occasionally? Option 1 or 3 is plenty — no point building something elaborate you rarely use.
Recommendation. This one genuinely depends on how often you bill many sites at once — which is easier to judge after it's in real use. That's why it's parked. Tell us "we bill 20+ sites monthly" and it's Option 2; if it's rare, leave it manual.
