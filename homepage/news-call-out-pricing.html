Pricing call-outs fairly: a simple framework for out-of-hours rates, travel and minimum charges

Customers accept higher prices at 2am. What they will not accept is a surprise.

11 / 09 / 20264 min readArdent team

Emergency work is where small trade businesses either make good money or quietly lose it. The difference is usually not the rate itself but how clearly it is set and explained.

Four parts of a fair call-out price

  • Attendance charge. A fixed fee for turning up, which covers the first period on site.
  • Time bands. Clear rates for evenings, nights, weekends and bank holidays.
  • Travel. Either built into the attendance charge or priced by distance, but never both.
  • Minimum charge. So a five-minute fix still covers the cost of getting out of bed.

Say it before you set off

Read the price to the customer on the phone and send it in writing before you leave. It takes thirty seconds and prevents most disputes.

How Ardent helps

Set your bands once and Ardent prices every emergency job by the time it is booked, sends the customer a confirmation with the charges, and carries them through to the invoice.
